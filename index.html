Red flags in the pitch

  • Unsolicited contact. A stranger claimed to have evaluated your engineering profile.
  • Unusually high compensation. The offer was designed to overpower your skepticism.
  • No real interview. A provisional job offer arrived before any meaningful screening.
  • Artificial urgency. “Reserved slot” and “24 hours” pushed you toward acting before verifying.
  • Early identity and payroll questions. Even optional verification during initial outreach is a warning sign.
  • Impressive-looking letter. Attractive design and a signature do not establish legitimacy.

How to verify a real recruiter

  • Find the position independently. Check the employer's own careers site, not just the supplied link.
  • Confirm the recruiter's identity. Contact the company using publicly listed channels and verify email domains carefully.
  • Keep sensitive details back. Don't provide government IDs, SSNs, or bank information before a legitimate, verified offer and secure onboarding.
  • Examine the form. Look for questionable domains, missing privacy notices, or unusually broad data requests.
  • Question off-platform pressure. Be skeptical of immediate moves to encrypted chats, payment requests, or “equipment checks.”
  • Report abuse. Flag fake listings to the platform and report suspected fraud to relevant authorities.
